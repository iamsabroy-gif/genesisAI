## Section 2: Architectural & Technological Foundations (Deep Technical Mechanics)

### Ethanol Blending Mechanism
Ethanol is blended with gasoline to form a gasohol mixture. This process involves adding a specific percentage of ethanol to gasoline. The most common ethanol-blended gasoline is E85, which contains 85% ethanol and 15% gasoline. The blending process can be done at the refinery or at the fuel pump.

### Core Technical Differentiators
- **Lower Emissions:** Ethanol is a cleaner-burning fuel, containing fewer carbon atoms than gasoline, which reduces the amount of pollutants emitted.
- **Sustainability:** Ethanol can be produced from renewable resources such as corn, sugar cane, and wheat, making it a more sustainable fuel option.
- **Lower Energy Density:** Ethanol has a lower energy density than gasoline, which means that the fuel efficiency of vehicles using ethanol-blended gasoline is slightly lower.
- **Infrastructure:** Ethanol fueling infrastructure is still developing, which can be a barrier to widespread adoption.
